**Recent advances in pharmacognosy (2025–2026) emphasize the integration of artificial intelligence, multi-omics, nanotechnology, and molecular tools with traditional natural-product research.** The field is shifting toward more predictive, sustainable, and systems-oriented approaches for discovering, standardizing, and developing bioactive compounds from plants, microbes, and marine sources.

### Molecular Pharmacognosy and Genomic Approaches
Molecular pharmacognosy, now three decades old, has matured into a core interdisciplinary discipline. Key achievements include standardized molecular identification of crude drugs (e.g., DNA barcoding), elucidation of mechanisms underlying geo-authentic (“Dao-di”) herbs, conservation models for rare resources, and biosynthetic pathway mapping of active compounds. Future directions explicitly include deeper fusion with AI, pan-omics, gene editing, biomanufacturing, and smart breeding to move from recognition of natural products toward prediction and rational design.

### AI, Machine Learning, and Multi-Omics Integration
AI is transforming nearly every stage of natural-product discovery:
- Tools for plant identification, extraction optimization, metabolite dereplication (via LC-MS/NMR analysis), ADMET prediction, virtual screening, network pharmacology, and generative design of pseudo-natural products.
- Linked mass spectrometry–genomics datasets combined with transformer models enable prioritization of microbial producers with high precision.
- Multi-omics (genomics, metabolomics, etc.) integration with AI models metabolic networks in medicinal plants and supports reverse pharmacology or systems-level understanding of multi-component herbal formulas.
- Applications range from predicting antiplasmodial activity across thousands of species to decoding mechanisms of traditional formulas (e.g., for cough-variant asthma).

These methods address longstanding bottlenecks in data complexity, silent biosynthetic gene clusters, and the multi-target nature of natural products.

### Botanical Drugs, Entourage Effect, and Formulations
There is renewed focus on whole-plant or multi-compound extracts that leverage the “entourage effect” (synergistic interactions among constituents), supported by advances in analytical chemistry for chemical fingerprinting and standardization. This aligns with growing regulatory pathways for botanical drugs. Nanophytomedicine continues to expand, with nanoformulations (liposomes, phytosomes, solid lipid nanoparticles) improving solubility, bioavailability, and targeted delivery of phytochemicals such as curcumin. Green synthesis approaches convert invasive plants (e.g., *Mikania micrantha*) into silver nanoparticles that serve as catalysts for drug intermediates while showing antibacterial and anticancer activity.

### Analytical, Marine, and Sustainability Advances
High-throughput phytochemical profiling (e.g., HPLC–DAD/ELSD of hundreds of species) is identifying candidates for cosmetics and therapeutics, with cultivation optimization enhancing bioactivity. Green liquid-phase microextraction techniques are refining analysis of complex traditional Chinese medicine samples. Marine natural products and microbial metagenomics remain active frontiers for novel scaffolds, with AI guidelines emerging to accelerate marine biopharmaceutical development. Sustainability themes include resource conservation, use of invasive species, and environmentally friendly processing.

**Overall trajectory**: Pharmacognosy is evolving from classical isolation and characterization toward an AI-augmented, multi-omics, and systems-biology discipline that supports precision herbal medicines, botanical drugs, and sustainable bioprospecting. Challenges remain in data standardization, translational validation, and equitable access to biodiversity resources. Leading journals include *Pharmacognosy Magazine*, *Journal of Natural Products*, *Natural Products and Bioprospecting*, and related natural-product chemistry outlets.
