**Recent advances in integrative health and medicine (2025–2026) emphasize evidence-based implementation of whole-person approaches, virtual delivery models, integration of traditional systems with AI, and expanded use in oncology and chronic pain.** Adoption is growing in health systems despite ongoing challenges around payment, workforce, and evidence standards.

### Clinical Implementation and Outcomes
- The U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs’ **Whole Health** model (combining primary care with mindfulness, acupuncture, nutritional counseling, wellness coaching, and app-based tools) improved pain interference more than cognitive behavioral therapy or usual care at 12 months in a large randomized trial of veterans with chronic pain. Prior data also linked it to opioid dose reductions.
- **Virtual integrative programs** are showing strong results. Memorial Sloan Kettering’s Integrative Medicine at Home (IM@Home)—featuring yoga, fitness, and meditation classes—improved quality of life, reduced hospitalizations and ED visits, and was associated with higher estimated 2-year overall survival in cancer patients compared with usual care.
- Large-scale pragmatic trials are advancing mind-body practices into mainstream care. A multi-site implementation study of virtual tai chi for chronic knee pain is underway across UCLA Health and other major systems, building on earlier efficacy research for depression, balance, and joint health.
- Institutional consolidation is occurring: UCLA Health has unified its integrative offerings (acupuncture, yoga, meditation, East-West medicine, lifestyle programs, oncology support) under a new Collaborative to improve coordination, research, and access.

Surveys of health system leaders indicate that integrative services are prevalent in roughly 60% of organizations, with most reporting better patient outcomes, experience, trust, and equity; use has increased in about half of settings over recent years. Common offerings include mental health support, nutrition, exercise, mindfulness, and acupuncture.

### Integrative Oncology
Updated guidelines from the Society for Integrative Oncology (SIO) and American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) provide evidence-based recommendations for managing anxiety, depression, pain, and fatigue with modalities such as mindfulness-based interventions (high-level evidence), yoga, acupuncture, and relaxation therapies during active treatment and survivorship. Programs continue to expand symptom management, quality of life, and supportive care alongside conventional therapies.

### Traditional Medicine, AI, and Global Strategies
The WHO’s Global Traditional Medicine Strategy 2025–2034 promotes integration of traditional, complementary, and integrative medicine (TCIM) into health systems, with goals around evidence generation, safety regulation, workforce development, and knowledge libraries. This has prompted debate: supporters highlight access and cultural relevance, while critics argue it risks lowering evidentiary standards by accepting observational or real-world data in place of rigorous randomized trials.

AI is accelerating applications in traditional systems:
- WHO, ITU, and WIPO reports map AI uses for diagnostics (e.g., Ayurgenomics), medicinal plant identification, compound analysis, and herb-drug interaction prediction.
- National initiatives include India’s AI + AYUSH Ministry partnership for datasets and innovation, and frameworks for deeper TCM–Western medicine integration via systems biology, multi-omics, and AI-driven pattern recognition or formula optimization.

Other trends include personalized diagnostics and treatment plans, microbiome-focused strategies, hormone optimization as preventive care, and tools such as AI-supported medicinal diet recommenders.

### Challenges and Outlook
Barriers remain in reimbursement, training pipelines, equitable access, and generating high-quality evidence suited to complex, multi-component interventions. Summits (e.g., UAE Al Shifaa, India’s Illness to Wellness) and institutional efforts continue to push research, education, and policy alignment. Overall, integrative approaches are shifting from peripheral options toward structured, evidence-informed components of mainstream and value-based care, particularly for chronic conditions, symptoms, and whole-person wellness.
