**Recent advances in botany and plant science (2025–2026) center on AI-driven analysis of vast digitized collections, breakthroughs in synthetic biology for crop improvement, accelerated genomics across the plant tree of life, and continued discovery of new species amid rising extinction risks.** These developments address climate resilience, food security, biodiversity conservation, and fundamental plant biology.

### Digitization, AI, and Conservation
The Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew’s sixth *State of the World’s Plants and Fungi* report (2026) highlights how AI and digitization are transforming efforts to document and protect plant and fungal diversity. Kew has digitized its entire 7.4 million herbarium and fungarium specimens. AI analysis of millions of these records revealed that flowering times have shifted by an average of 2.5 days per decade over the past century. High-quality genomes are now being recovered from specimens up to 180 years old, turning historical collections into resources for medicine, crop protection, and disease prediction. Thousands of new plant and fungal species were formally named in 2024–2025 (including a “zombie” fungus parasitizing Brazilian spiders and various orchids and “living stones”), though many undescribed species are already threatened.

Institutions such as the New York Botanical Garden are advancing AI initiatives for climate resilience, food-plant conservation, and unlocking herbarium data, alongside discovering dozens of new species annually.

### Synthetic Biology, Genome Editing, and Crop Improvement
Key engineering advances include:
- Near-complete synthetic apomixis in hybrid rice (via the transcription factor HUAXU), enabling clonal seed production that fixes hybrid vigor with minimal yield penalty.
- De novo design of synthetic plant immune receptors that can be refined to detect diverse pathogen proteins.
- Gene edits and modifications boosting yield (up to 20–25% in rice), drought/heat tolerance, lignin content (for biomass), growth, and disease resistance—often by altering plant-native genes or regulatory regions.
- High-throughput tools such as ENTRAP-seq for rapidly screening thousands of plant gene regulators, and automated robotic systems for phenotyping multicellular plant responses to optimize growth under climate stressors.

The European Union adopted Regulation (EU) 2026/1388 on plants obtained by certain new genomic techniques, updating frameworks for modern breeding methods.

### Genomics and Fundamental Insights
The proposed PLANeT initiative aims to generate high-quality, standardized genomes across the land-plant tree of life, integrating AI to decode conserved principles for biology, conservation, crop improvement, and natural-product discovery. Parallel progress includes molecular details of hormone signaling (e.g., brassinosteroids enhancing sugar synthesis), photoprotection mechanisms, potassium channels controlling petiole growth, systemin receptors in wound responses, and evolutionary insights from non-seed plants (bryophytes, ferns, charophytes). Chinese plant scientists reported major gains in publications on stress adaptation, immunity, reproductive biology, and evolution-informed breeding.

**Overall trajectory**: Botany is increasingly data-intensive, AI-augmented, and application-oriented, linking classical taxonomy and ecology with synthetic biology and precision breeding to tackle climate change, biodiversity loss, and food security. Challenges include the slow pace of species description relative to extinction rates and ensuring equitable, sustainable use of genomic resources. Leading outlets include *Nature Plants*, *The Plant Cell*, *Plant Biotechnology Journal*, and institutional reports from Kew and major botanical gardens.
