**Recent advances in quantum biology (2025–2026) focus on quantum sensing tools that operate inside living cells, refined evidence for quantum effects in photosynthesis and magnetoreception, and the emergence of “quantum life science” as an interdisciplinary paradigm linking fundamental mechanisms to biomedical applications.** The field is maturing from theoretical speculation toward experimentally testable tools and clearer evidence maps.

### Quantum Sensing and Genetically Encoded Qubits
A major thrust is developing sensors that exploit quantum properties (coherence, spin) for nanoscale measurements in biological environments:
- Fluorescent proteins (e.g., enhanced yellow fluorescent protein) have been engineered as optically addressable spin qubits that can be genetically expressed inside living cells (demonstrated in human cells and *E. coli*). These “quantum proteins” enable magnetic resonance-style measurements of local fields or temperatures without relying solely on synthetic nanoparticles.
- Biocompatible molecular quantum nanosensors and nanodiamond probes measure temperature and chemical radicals with subcellular resolution, revealing thermal “hot spots” inside cell nuclei and linking sensor signals to cellular activity.
- Advances address practical hurdles such as zero-field splitting shifts in diamond sensors inside cells (shown to originate from surface effects rather than bulk properties) and enable simultaneous control of solid-state and biological qubits in the same live cell via pulsed quantum microscopes.
- The U.S. National Science Foundation renewed the Quantum Leap Challenge Institute for Quantum Sensing for Biophysics and Bioengineering (QuBBE) with $37.5 million over five years to advance nanoprobes, entanglement-enhanced sensing, in vivo measurements, and biomedical translation. Related centers for quantum biology and medicine are forming.

Frameworks now classify quantum biomedical sensors into generations based on the quantum resources they use (from discrete levels to coherence, entanglement, and adaptive quantum learning).

### Quantum Effects in Core Biological Processes
- **Photosynthesis**: Full microscopic (non-perturbative) simulations of the Fenna–Matthews–Olson complex demonstrate long-lived excitonic coherences persisting on picosecond timescales at both cryogenic and room temperature—timescales relevant to energy transfer. Approximate models had previously led some to dismiss these effects; the new results underscore the need for realistic vibrational environments in interpreting experimental spectra.
- **Magnetoreception and radical pairs**: The radical-pair mechanism remains a leading candidate for avian (and some plant) magnetic sensing. Studies explore chirality-induced spin selectivity, quantum Zeno effects, and responses under hypomagnetic fields, including impacts on reactive oxygen species in plants.
- Enzymatic proton/hydrogen tunneling continues as one of the more firmly established quantum contributions under physiological conditions.

Comprehensive evidence-mapping reviews distinguish three directions—**quantum in biology** (effects inside living systems), **quantum for biology** (quantum tools applied to biological questions), and **biology for quantum** (biomolecules improving quantum devices)—while noting that many high-profile claims still require decisive tests against classical alternatives.

### Broader Paradigm and Translational Outlook
“Quantum Life Science” integrates nanoscale quantum biosensors, hyperpolarized MRI/NMR, and quantum biology to advance diagnostics, drug discovery, and understanding of processes linked to aging, cancer, and metabolism. Analogical uses of quantum concepts (e.g., discrete stable states in cancer cell populations) are also appearing, though these do not claim underlying quantum mechanics at the atomic scale.

**Overall trajectory**: Quantum biology is shifting toward robust, genetically encodable sensors and higher-fidelity simulations that can operate under warm, wet, noisy biological conditions. Key challenges include distinguishing true quantum contributions from classical or vibronic effects, achieving practical sensitivity and biocompatibility, and translating tools into medicine. Major venues include *Nature*, *Science Advances*, *ACS Nano*, arXiv reviews, and dedicated workshops such as QuEBS.
