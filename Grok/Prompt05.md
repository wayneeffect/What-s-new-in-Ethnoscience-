**Recent advances in pharmacology (2025–2026) center on the clinical maturation of targeted protein degradation, AI integration across pharmacokinetics/pharmacodynamics and systems modeling, novel mechanisms for previously challenging targets, and a shift toward human-relevant New Approach Methodologies (NAMs).** These developments expand the druggable proteome, improve precision, and address resistance and undruggable biology.

### Targeted Protein Degradation (TPD)
A major milestone is the FDA approval of the first engineered PROTAC (proteolysis-targeting chimera), Vepdegestrant (ARV-471/Veppanu), which harnesses the ubiquitin-proteasome system to degrade disease-causing proteins. Shortly after, Iberdomide (Zenbexus), the first CELMoD molecular glue degrader, was approved for multiple myeloma.

These event-driven modalities (versus traditional occupancy-based inhibition) enable degradation of scaffolding proteins, transcription factors, and resistance-prone targets. Research is advancing AI-designed PROTACs (e.g., for PKMYT1 or fusion proteins), recyclable LYTACs, expanded E3 ligase recruitment, metabolism/biotransformation studies of degraders, and combinations with radioligands or other modalities. Challenges remain in pharmacokinetics, tissue selectivity, and linker design.

### AI, Systems Pharmacology, and Computational Tools
AI is reshaping core pharmacological processes:
- Machine learning and hybrid models improve individualized drug exposure prediction (PK), effect modeling with interindividual variability (PD), adaptive dosing, and adverse-event signal detection from unstructured data (pharmacovigilance).
- Quantitative systems pharmacology (QSP) integrates with generative AI, digital twins, and retrieval-augmented systems for better model generation, virtual patients, and predictive power.
- Network pharmacology and polypharmacology leverage graph neural networks and knowledge graphs for multi-target strategies. Agentic AI and large language models support target prioritization, molecular design, and closed-loop discovery workflows.
- Updated resources include the *BJP Concise Guide to Pharmacology 2025/26*, summarizing ~1,900 human drug targets and thousands of ligand interactions.

### Novel Targets, Mechanisms, and Approvals
In 2025, dozens of novel drugs (plus cellular/gene therapies) were approved, with oncology leading via biomarker-defined subtypes. Eleven novel mechanism-of-action targets appeared among first-in-class agents.

Notable mechanistic advances include:
- Strategies for KRAS(Q61) mutants that restore GTPase activity and favor the inactive state.
- Emerging CDK2 inhibitors (cyclic peptides, allosteric ligands) beyond established CDK4/6 agents.
- Next-generation TYK2 inhibitors advancing in late-stage development.
- Allosteric modulators (e.g., for drug-resistant epilepsy or Parkinson’s-related pathways).
- New antibiotics such as gepotidacin (distinct topoisomerase inhibition) addressing resistance.
- SLC6A19 (B0AT1) inhibitors for phenylketonuria and metabolic conditions.

Psychiatry pipelines feature multi-mechanism agents (e.g., AXS-05 for Alzheimer’s agitation) and advancing psychedelics. Rare-disease therapies, such as elamipretide for Barth syndrome (mitochondrial targeting), continue to emerge.

### Preclinical Innovation and Broader Trends
NAMs—iPSC models, organoids, microphysiological systems, and AI—are gaining regulatory traction (supported by FDA Modernization Acts) to reduce animal testing and improve human predictivity for efficacy and toxicity. Metabolism research is adapting to complex new modalities (PROTACs, oligonucleotides, macrocyclic peptides, ADCs/conjugates).

**Overall trajectory**: Pharmacology is moving from single-target inhibition toward degradation, multi-target/network modulation, and data-driven personalization. Convergence of AI, novel modalities, and human-centric models promises higher success rates against resistant or complex diseases, though challenges in clinical translation, long-term safety of degraders, and equitable access persist. Key sources include *Nature Chemical Biology*, *Nature Reviews Drug Discovery*, *British Journal of Pharmacology*, *Drug Metabolism Reviews*, and conference disclosures (e.g., ACS).
